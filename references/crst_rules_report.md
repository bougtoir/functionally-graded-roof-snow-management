## Verified requirements

### 1. Aims and scope

- **[E]** CRST covers scientific and technical problems of cold environments in polar and temperate locations, including fundamental cryospheric science with cold-regions applications and engineering topics related to the cryosphere.
- **[E]** Its emphasized subjects are the physical and mechanical aspects of ice (including glaciers and sea ice), snow and avalanches, ice–water systems, ice-bonded soils, and permafrost. Earth science, materials science, offshore and river-ice engineering, icing of ships/structures, trafficability, and cold-regions technological advances are also in scope.
- **[E]** Theoretical papers must include “a detailed discussion of the potential application of the theory to address cold regions problems.”
- **[I]** A roof-snow-management paper is facially within the stated “snow and snow avalanche” and applied cold-regions engineering scope, but actual suitability remains an editorial decision.

### 2. Article types and review

- **[E]** Permitted regular article types are **Research Articles, Review Articles, and Short Communications**.
- **[E]** The journal uses **single-anonymized review**; suitable papers are typically assessed by at least two independent reviewers.
- **[I]** The current CRST Guide does not state body-word, page, figure-count, table-count, or reference-count limits for these article types. This is an absence in the current published Guide, not an affirmative guarantee that Editorial Manager will impose no field/file limits.

### 3. Manuscript preparation

- **[E] Source files:** Submit editable files for the entire submission. Word must be `.doc`/`.docx`; LaTeX must be `.tex`. A PDF is **not** an acceptable source file. Word manuscripts must use a single-column layout; double-column layout is permitted only for LaTeX.
- **[E] Title page:** Include a concise informative title; author names in submission-system order; full affiliations and postal addresses with country; one clearly identified corresponding author and email; and present/permanent-address footnotes where applicable.
- **[E] Abstract:** Concise and factual, **maximum 250 words**; state purpose, basic procedures, main findings (including statistical significance where applicable), principal conclusions, and novel/important aspects. It must stand alone; avoid references and undefined nonstandard abbreviations.
- **[E] Keywords:** **1–7**, in English; avoid multiword terms where possible and use abbreviations only if established.
- **[E] Highlights:** **Required** at submission as a **separate editable file**, with “highlights” in the filename; **3–5 bullets**, each no more than **85 characters including spaces**.
- **[E] Graphical abstract:** Encouraged, not stated as mandatory. Upload separately; image should be **531 × 1328 pixels (h × w) or proportionally larger**, readable at 5 × 13 cm; preferred formats: TIFF, EPS, PDF, or MS Office.
- **[E] Structure:** Divide the manuscript into clearly defined sections. Put acknowledgements in a separate section immediately before the references, not on the title page or in a footnote.
- **[E] CRediT:** The corresponding author is required to report co-author contributions using the CRediT taxonomy.
- **[E] Line numbering:** The submission checklist requires **continuous line numbers throughout the manuscript, from title through the end of figure/table captions**.

### 4. Tables, figures, and supplementary files

- **[E] Tables:** Submit as editable text, not images. They may be placed beside the relevant text or on separate pages at the end. Cite all tables; number in order of appearance; supply captions and notes; avoid vertical rules and cell shading.
- **[E] Figures/artwork:** Supply figures and other artwork as **separate files** with logical names such as `Figure_1`; cite and number them in order. Text graphics may be embedded in the manuscript. Every artwork item needs a caption with a brief title and description, explaining symbols/abbreviations.
- **[E] Artwork formats/resolution:**
  - Vector drawings: EPS or PDF; embed fonts or save text as graphics.
  - Photographs: TIFF/JPG/PNG, minimum **300 dpi**; minimum width 1063 px (single column) or 2244 px (full page).
  - Bitmapped line drawings: TIFF/JPG/PNG, minimum **1000 dpi**; minimum width 3543 px (single column) or 7480 px (full page).
  - Charts/large or shaded tables: XLS/XLSX, clearly readable.
  - Annotated images: PPT/PPTX; legible text; embedded images minimum 300 dpi.
- **[E]** Do not submit low-resolution artwork, disproportionately small labels, or combined multi-image/graph files unless comparison requires the combination.
- **[E] Supplementary material:** Cite it in the manuscript and submit it with the initial manuscript. Supply a concise caption for each file. It is published as received and is not production-edited; after initial submission it may be added/replaced only at revision.

### 5. References

- **[E] Submission-stage flexibility:** CRST imposes no strict reference-format requirement at initial submission, provided one style is used consistently and complete bibliographic data are supplied. The journal style is applied after acceptance.
- **[E] Target journal style:** In-text citations are author–year: one author `(Author, year)`, two authors `(Author and Author, year)`, and three or more `(Author et al., year)`. The reference list is alphabetical, then chronological where needed; same-author/same-year works use a/b/c. Journal names are abbreviated according to LTWA.
- **[E] Integrity/completeness:** Every in-text citation must be in the reference list and vice versa; every reference must be a real source; include DOI where available.
- **[E] Data/software citations:** The Guide gives explicit dataset and software reference examples. Dataset citations should include authors, title, repository, version where available, year, persistent identifier, and `[dataset]` immediately before the reference.

### 6. Research data and code

- **[E]** CRST applies Elsevier research-data **Option C**. Authors are required to (1) deposit research data in a relevant repository and (2) cite and link the dataset in the article; if sharing is not possible, explain why.
- **[E]** A **data-availability statement is required at submission** and is published with the article. Restricted/sensitive data may be justified in the submission statement.
- **[E]** “Research data” expressly includes software, code, models, algorithms, protocols, methods, and other project materials in addition to observations/experimental results.
- **[E]** Provide the repository link when prompted in submission and use persistent identifiers in the manuscript where appropriate.
- **[I]** For a computational roof-snow study, the safest compliance package is a versioned archival release of analysis/simulation code plus the exact input and result data needed to verify findings, each with a persistent identifier, cited in the manuscript. The Guide does not prescribe GitHub, Zenodo, or a specific license.

### 7. Declarations and ethics

- **[E] Submission declaration:** Submission implies that the work is not previously published except allowed forms (e.g., preprint, abstract, lecture, thesis, registered report), is not under consideration elsewhere, is approved by all authors/responsible authorities, and will not be republished in the same form without copyright-holder consent.
- **[E] Competing interests:** All authors must disclose financial/personal relationships. The Elsevier declarations tool “should always be completed”; authors with none select “I have nothing to declare.” Upload the resulting declaration as a `.doc`/`.docx` file at the attach/upload-files step; signatures are not required.
- **[E] Funding:** Identify funders/grant numbers and state funder roles in design, data collection/analysis/interpretation, writing, and submission decision. If unfunded, Elsevier recommends: “This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors.”
- **[E] Authorship:** Authors must substantially contribute to conception/design or data acquisition/analysis/interpretation, drafting or critical revision, and final approval; all are accountable. A single corresponding author must be appointed.

### 8. Generative AI

- **[E] Manuscript preparation:** AI may support preparation only with human oversight; it cannot replace critical thinking or evaluation. Authors remain responsible for accuracy, completeness, impartiality, originality, source verification, privacy, IP, and tool terms.
- **[E] Disclosure:** Disclose manuscript-preparation AI in a separate declaration at submission, naming the tool/service, purpose, and author oversight. The CRST Guide places the section before the references. Basic grammar/spelling/reference checks do not require disclosure; substantive changes to sentence structure or organization do.
- **[E] Research use:** AI used as part of research must be described reproducibly in Methods, including tool/model name, version, and developer/manufacturer where applicable.
- **[E] Authorship:** AI tools cannot be authors/co-authors and should not be cited as authors.
- **[E] Images:** AI may support explanatory images if disclosed both in the caption (tool, version, use) and general AI statement. AI-supported data visualization is allowed only when faithfully and reproducibly derived from underlying data; disclose the tool/model, version, and developer in Methods. AI must not fabricate/alter data or create/alter primary research images not directly obtained in the research.
- **[E] Graphical abstracts:** Elsevier’s current detailed policy says **general-purpose generative-AI image tools must not be used to create graphical abstracts**; dedicated scientific illustration tools are encouraged.

### 9. Submission files and online submission

- **[E]** Upload all required editable manuscript files, separate artwork, videos, supplements, tables/footnotes, and captions. Editorial Manager converts article files to one PDF for peer review, but editable files remain required for production.
- **[E]** Checklist items include designated corresponding author with current details, spell/grammar checks, bidirectional reference checking, permissions for copyrighted third-party material, and continuous line numbering.
- **[E]** The Guide does not state a mandatory reporting checklist (e.g., CONSORT/STROBE) for all CRST articles; use a study-design-specific checklist if applicable.

### 10. Cover letter

- **[E—journal-specific finding]** The current CRST Guide contains **no cover-letter section or journal-specific cover-letter content rule**.
- **[E—generic Elsevier guidance, not CRST-specific]** Elsevier nevertheless says to include a short, focused cover letter (ideally under one page) stating the study aim/main findings, fit with journal aims/scope, novelty and broader implications, and any invitation to submit. Do **not** put funding, author declarations, or reviewer suggestions/oppositions in the letter. Elsevier notes that an individual journal may require a letter and directs authors to its Guide.
- **[I]** Prepare the generic one-page Elsevier letter, but treat mandatory upload status as unverified until the live CRST Editorial Manager submission workflow displays its file requirements.
