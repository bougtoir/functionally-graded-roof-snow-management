# Verified CRST literature: roof snow load, deposition, shedding, and control

## Scope and verification standard

This is a focused—not exhaustive—set of *Cold Regions Science and Technology* (CRST) papers with direct relevance to roof snow or clearly labeled adjacent relevance to sliding/adhesion/control. Bibliographic fields were independently matched against Crossref and Elsevier core metadata. Claims below are limited to publisher abstracts/highlights or authoritative institutional copies/summaries. **High** confidence means both metadata and the stated claim were directly supported; **Medium** is used once where the CRST metadata is direct but the methodological interpretation is triangulated through the authors’ companion paper rather than a retrievable CRST abstract.

## Evidence synthesis

- **Roof geometry and wind jointly control deposition.** Large-scale measurements found larger surrounding drifts for a flat roof than a single-pitch roof and larger drifts for an upstream-tilted than downstream-tilted single-pitch roof. Flat-roof wind-tunnel tests further located the usual maximum near the windward region, with a possible rear peak on large spans.
- **CFD is useful only with empirical validation and explicit model limits.** The CRST review identifies strong potential for roof-snow prediction but calls for validation databases, practical guidance, and continued reliance on observations. Applied studies demonstrate validated multiphase approaches and coupling of simulated nonuniform snow load to structural analysis.
- **Meteorological load models should not reduce roof snow to precipitation alone.** Wind-dominated deposition and transport can create unbalanced loads; a northern-China analysis explicitly treats joint snowfall, wind, and temperature indicators.
- **Slope and surface energy affect shedding, but adjacent evidence must not be overstated.** Monoslope experiments separate lower-slope (0–10°) from higher-slope (15–25°) drift behavior. The PV study supports snow transmittance and transmitted solar radiation as sliding/melting predictors; it is not a general roof-snow-load validation.
- **Adhesion evidence is mechanistically relevant but not roof-specific.** On metallic surfaces, liquid-water content and roughness strongly affect adhesion; wet snow showed higher shear adhesion but lower tensile adhesion than dry snow, consistent with capillary-dominated tensile behavior.
- **The verified CRST control paper is active, not passive.** The automatic membrane-roof system uses electrical heating wires and thermal control. It must not be cited as evidence for passive or functionally graded snow shedding.

## Critical gap for a passive/functionally graded roof claim

No paper in the verified CRST set directly tests a **passive, functionally graded roof surface** for snow shedding or load reduction. The defensible literature bridge is limited to: (1) physical roof deposition/load studies; (2) adjacent PV sliding/melting parameters; and (3) metallic-surface adhesion mechanics. The electrical–thermal membrane roof is an active-control comparator and must not be characterized as passive. A manuscript should therefore present passive functional grading as the study’s unresolved design hypothesis/contribution, not as an established CRST result.
