# Retention and non-shedding roof literature audit

## Verified evidence

All 22 retained literature and guidance records resolve to a checksum-verified
local source snapshot. DOI records were checked against retained Crossref work
metadata; official Japanese guides and administrative guidance were checked
against the retained source files.

The audit corrected the Sapporo guidance year to its documented 2025 update and
added Watanabe and Takita (1997), volume 13, issue 1, pages 3-12, DOI
10.4106/jsse.13.3.

Two retained metadata variants are documented rather than silently copied:

where the article title and subject require “SNOW.”

## Fair comparator context

Japanese sources describe multiple conditional roof-snow strategies rather
than one universally preferable solution:

- Retention or non-shedding roofs can reduce the snow-fall space required at
  dense sites and reduce routine eave-release exposure.
- Retained snow remains a long-duration load, so structural capacity,
  drainage, waterproofing, inspection, and maintenance remain essential.
- Shedding roofs can reduce retained mass but require adequate receiving
  space and controls for falling snow and ice.
- Active or manual removal and hybrid systems can be appropriate where neither
  passive extreme is acceptable.
- Sapporo guidance accepts several context-dependent measures and explicitly
  states that administrative confirmation does not guarantee zero falling
  snow or ice.

## Consequence for this study

The study asks how spatial grading changes a modeled retained-mass versus
discrete-release trade-off under identical forcing. It does not show that
shedding is intrinsically desirable, that retention roofs are unsafe, or that
graded roofs are universally preferable. The model omits structural response,
drainage, wind redistribution, cornice formation, receiving-zone exposure,
maintenance, and site geometry. Those omissions prevent a safety or
whole-building ranking.

## Geographic boundary

Japanese municipal and regional guidance supports the existence and rationale
of retention practice in snowy Japanese contexts. It is not generalized as a
global building requirement or universal construction specification.
