# JAPAN_CONTEXT_EVIDENCE

All statements below are traced to official sources stored in `data/raw/japan_context/20261109/` (ledger topic `r1_japan_context`). Numerical items are tabulated in `JAPAN_SNOW_POPULATION_EVIDENCE.csv`; legal items in `JAPAN_REGULATORY_MATRIX.csv`.

## 1. Snow and population
- Heavy snowfall areas designated under the Act on Special Measures for Heavy Snowfall Areas cover 532 of 1,719 municipalities and 50.8 % of national land, and contain 18.25 million residents (14.5 % of the population, 2020 census); special heavy snowfall areas hold 2.79 million (2.2 %). Hokkaido, Aomori and Niigata are among the ten prefectures designated in their entirety (MLIT, as of 2026-04-01).
- Large cities with heavy snowfall: 1991–2020 annual snowfall normals are 479 cm at Sapporo and 567 cm at Aomori (maximum snow depth normals 97 and 101 cm). Niigata City station is coastal (139 cm); inland Niigata stations such as Takada, already in the JMA daily dataset, are far snowier.

## 2. Burden and safety
- MLIT reports 22 snow-related deaths in the FY2023 winter despite low snowfall; over the previous ten winters about 90 % of snow-related deaths occurred during snow removal and about 90 % of those victims were aged 65 or older; falls from roofs and falling roof snow are the leading fatal accident types (MLIT, Feb 2025, citing FDMA data).
- The 7th Basic Plan (Cabinet decision 2022-12-09) identifies a shortage of snow-removal workers driven by population decline and ageing, and promotes snow-resistant housing and safe roof-snow handling.

## 3. Legal and technical frame (binding vs voluntary)
- Binding: Enforcement Order Art. 86 defines snow load from unit weight, projected area and regional vertical snow depth; local special administrative agencies designate heavy-snow districts and set depths; the slope reduction μb = √cos(1.5β) does **not** apply when snow stops are present; uneven snow must be considered; design depth may be reduced to 1 m where roof snow removal is customary, with a mandatory notice.
- Programme law: the Heavy Snowfall Areas Act designates areas and plans; it contains no roof design rules.
- Voluntary/administrative: MLIT safety rules for roof snow removal, lifeline anchors, municipal subsidies; snow guards and receiving space are practice, not national requirements.

## 4. How the manuscript uses this
- Motivation only; the model produces retained mass per metre of roof strip, not code design loads, and no claim of code compliance is made.
- Art. 86(4) explains why retention is not free: retaining snow forfeits the slope-based load reduction, so the retention–shedding trade-off has a direct structural-load counterpart.
- Not verified and therefore not stated: municipal design snow depths, local falling-snow ordinances, numbers of roof-snow removal workers.
